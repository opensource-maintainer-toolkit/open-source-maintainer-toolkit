# Community Building for Open Source Maintainers

A guide to growing and nurturing a contributor community around your project.

## Why Community Matters

A project with an active community gets bug reports faster, feature requests that match real needs, and code contributions that reduce the maintainer's workload. A project without one becomes a maintenance burden.

## Getting Started

1. **Label issues clearly.** Use `good first issue` for beginner tasks, `help wanted` for tasks needing contributors, and `documentation` for non-code work.
2. **Write a CONTRIBUTING.md.** Make it easy for someone to submit their first PR in under 30 minutes.
3. **Be responsive.** Reply to issues within 48 hours. Even a quick "thanks, looking at this" matters.

## Growing the Community

- **Participate in events.** Hacktoberfest, Drips Waves, and similar programs bring in new contributors.
- **Mentor contributors.** Help someone go from `good first issue` to regular contributor by reviewing their PRs carefully and offering feedback.
- **Recognize contributions.** Add contributors to a CONTRIBUTORS file, mention them in release notes.
- **Create a welcoming environment.** Enforce a Code of Conduct. Be kind in reviews.

## Retaining Contributors

- **Give ownership.** Let regular contributors triage issues or review PRs.
- **Share the roadmap.** Contributors who see where the project is going are more likely to stay.
- **Say thank you.** A genuine "thanks for this PR" goes a long way.

## Measuring Health

| Metric | Healthy | Concerning |
|--------|---------|------------|
| PR response time | < 48 hours | > 7 days |
| Issue close rate | Steady | Growing backlog |
| Unique contributors/month | Growing | Declining |
| Star:fork ratio | High (quality) | Low (interest without engagement) |
